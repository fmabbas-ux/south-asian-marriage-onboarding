# 00. Vision and Scope

## Problem

In most South Asian marriages, couples have little or no time together before the wedding, rarely live together first, and marry into a family as much as a person. Guidance covers the wedding day (venue, dowry, jewellery, photographers). Almost nothing covers the part after: sharing a room, adapting to a new household, negotiating money, handling parents and siblings, and defending the marriage from outside pressure.

A recent Dawn Images piece put the gap plainly: guides cover bedrooms and honeymoons, but "not a single one on how to adapt to a new family." A qualitative study of women-initiated divorce in Lahore (15 women) listed emotional neglect, violence, rigid gender roles, in-law interference and mismatched marriages as causes, and recommended premarital counselling and accessible support networks. This project is a practical answer to that recommendation.

## Audience

- **Primary:** the bride-to-be and groom-to-be, taught separately and together.
- **Secondary:** newlyweds in months 0 to 12.
- **Tertiary:** parents and siblings (a short "for families" track), and remarried couples with children.

## Core principles

1. **Both sides adapt.** The one-sided "bride adjusts" frame is the most common failure. Grooms face real strain too (torn loyalty, provider pressure, being the messenger between wife and mother). Content must speak to both.
2. **Situations over lectures.** Every idea is anchored in a concrete scene a viewer recognises ("Your mother-in-law reorganises your kitchen on day 3").
3. **Skills over information.** The premarital-education meta-analysis (Fawcett, Hawkins, Blanchard, Carroll, 2010) found programs reliably improved communication but showed mixed or modest effects on relationship satisfaction. So the course trains observable communication behaviours (scripts, role-plays, decision drills), not only awareness.
4. **Respect family, protect the couple.** Position the couple as a team that honours parents without being run by them. No content that mocks elders or religion.
5. **Religion-neutral core, with variants.** Core skills apply to every community. Legal and ritual modules branch: Pakistani Muslim (first), Indian Hindu/Sikh/Muslim/Christian, Bangladeshi, Sri Lankan, diaspora.
6. **Stated limits.** This is education, not therapy or legal advice. Abuse is not "conflict to be managed" (see `10-red-lines-and-safety.md`).

## In scope

Rishta stage conversations, engagement, nikah/wedding negotiations, the first 12 months of married life, parents/siblings/relatives, money, intimacy, careers, relocation, previous marriages and step-children, in-law living arrangements, mental health basics, early conflict repair, when to seek help.

## Out of scope (for now)

Fertility treatment details, detailed religious rulings, immigration/visa law, full legal advice, long-term parenting (beyond the first-baby pressure topic).

## Success measures

- Course: completion rate, couple-pair completion, pre/post skill self-ratings, 6 and 12 month follow-up survey.
- Game: sessions per couple, scenario agreement-gap scores (how differently each partner reads the same event).
- Repo: scenario count, contributor reviews, expert-review coverage per module.
