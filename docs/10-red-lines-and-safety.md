# 10. Red Lines and Safety

Content design rules, not generic disclaimers.

## Conflict is not abuse
The course teaches skills for **ordinary conflict** (differing habits, family friction, money disagreement). It must never teach "adjust more" or "be patient" as the answer to **abuse or coercive control**.

Treat the following as outside "conflict to manage":
- Physical violence or threats; sexual coercion
- Controlling money, movement, phone or contact with family as a pattern
- Confinement, taking documents, threats about children or divorce as control
- Persistent humiliation or intimidation
- Forced pregnancy, or forced termination

The course answer: **document, tell a trusted person, make a safety plan, get help**. Each country gets a local resources page (helplines, shelters, legal aid) maintained and reviewed regularly. Pakistan resource list to be compiled and verified before launch.

## Content guardrails
1. Do not frame one gender as the problem. Show both partners' view.
2. Do not shame divorce or praise it; describe options neutrally.
3. No religious rulings beyond what a qualified scholar has reviewed; offer scholar-reviewed notes where religion is invoked.
4. Legal content is general information, reviewed by a lawyer, dated, and updated.
5. Mental health content is educational. No diagnosis.
6. Avoid stereotypes of regions, castes, sects, professions and communities.
7. No real-person stories without written consent; anonymise.
8. Game and survey data: collected with consent, stored minimal, never shown to families.

## Review process
Each scenario and lesson carries `reviewed_by` and `reviewed_on`. Nothing in the legal, abuse or mental-health modules ships without a named reviewer.
