# 05. Scenario Library

Forty situations written for videos and the game. Each has: **setup**, **tension**, and **teaching point**. IDs map to `data/scenarios.yaml`. "D#" refers to the dynamic in `02-dynamics.md`.

## D1: The couple

1. **S01 Night one.** After a 14-hour wedding, one partner expects intimacy, the other expects sleep. Teaches: talk before, no performance pressure, consent.
2. **S02 Different tidiness.** His clothes on the chair, her cupboard colour-coded. Week 3, tension. Teaches: explicit standards, negotiate zones.
3. **S03 The phone.** She sees a message from an old friend; he sees her unlocking his phone. Teaches: privacy agreements, trust.
4. **S04 Silent treatment.** Argument at dinner; he goes quiet for two days. Teaches: pursue-withdraw pattern, 20-minute break and return.
5. **S05 Who cooks?** Her mother-in-law says "bahu cooks"; she has a full-time job. Teaches: roles decided before, not by default.
6. **S06 Prayer and practice gap.** One is strictly observant, the other relaxed. Teaches: respect without policing, shared minimum.
7. **S07 First fight about money.** He spends on his brothers without telling her. Teaches: transparency and the monthly money meeting.

## D2: Parents

8. **S08 The 7 a.m. knock.** Mother-in-law enters the couple's room unannounced. Teaches: privacy boundary, tone.
9. **S09 "Meri beti ko tang mat karna."** Her father calls hourly after hearing she looked sad. Teaches: parents' anxiety, handling calls.
10. **S10 Mother-in-law criticises cooking.** Every meal gets a comment. Teaches: translation by the husband, a role for the mother.
11. **S11 Whose Eid first?** Both families want the couple for day one. Teaches: alternating plan decided in advance.
12. **S12 The salary question.** In-laws expect his entire salary to go to the household; she wants some saved. Teaches: money structure, parental conversation script.
13. **S13 Dress code.** She is told what she can wear outside. Teaches: negotiating before the wedding, respect vs control.
14. **S14 Her mother as advisor.** Her mother tells her to hide money from her husband "for safety". Teaches: transparency and trust.
15. **S15 Father who says nothing.** Father-in-law silently approves of the mother's rules. Teaches: identifying the real decision maker.

## D3: Siblings

16. **S16 The nand who reports.** Everything she says reaches her mother-in-law. Teaches: information discipline.
17. **S17 The younger brother's tuition.** He expects the couple to pay. Teaches: a support limit, said as a couple.
18. **S18 The sister's rishta.** "Don't plan a baby until she is married." Teaches: refusing others' timelines.
19. **S19 The jealous sister.** Brother-in-law's wife competes over gifts. Teaches: not entering competition.
20. **S20 The house share.** Brothers argue over inheritance in front of the bride. Teaches: staying out, facts in writing.

## D4: Extended family

21. **S21 "Good news?"** Fourth month, every relative asks. Teaches: the three standard answers.
22. **S22 Weight and complexion comments.** At a dinner. Teaches: partner defence, humour, a prepared line.
23. **S23 The dominant phupho.** She sets the family opinion on the bride. Teaches: respectful relationship with influencers.
24. **S24 Every event.** The couple is expected at every function; he's exhausted. Teaches: choosing events.
25. **S25 The WhatsApp group.** A private quarrel shows up as a family story. Teaches: privacy and rumour control.

## D5: Ambitions

26. **S26 Job offer abroad.** Great offer, her parents are elderly, his parents want him close. Teaches: joint decision with a review date.
27. **S27 "Why do you need to work?"** In-laws oppose her job after the wedding. Teaches: pre-wedding agreement, calm firmness.
28. **S28 Wife earns more.** His family comments; he feels diminished. Teaches: roles, pride, partnership.
29. **S29 Business risk.** He wants to invest savings in a business. Teaches: risk limits, shared decision.
30. **S30 MBA vs baby.** Her study plan collides with family pressure on a child. Teaches: sequencing, shared plan.

## D6: External sources

31. **S31 Step-child.** Eight-year-old refuses to talk to the new wife. Teaches: patience, role clarity, support for the child.
32. **S32 The former spouse calls.** Custody questions arise at midnight. Teaches: contact rules, transparency.
33. **S33 The widower's first family.** Late wife's parents remain central. Teaches: honouring the past without a rival in the house.
34. **S34 The ex's like.** A social media interaction triggers distrust. Teaches: rules for online conduct.
35. **S35 The advice friend.** An unhappily married friend tells her to "keep him under control". Teaches: choosing confidants.
36. **S36 Second wife.** A man marries again, his first family is hurt, and the law requires steps. Teaches: legal and ethical duties, and what the new wife should ask before saying yes.

## Cross-cutting

37. **S37 Post-wedding low mood.** She feels sad and guilty about it. Teaches: normalising, naming, seeking help.
38. **S38 The fight in front of relatives.** He snaps at her in public. Teaches: repair, apology, private resolution.
39. **S39 When elders mediate.** A trusted uncle is asked to help. Teaches: how to choose a mediator, ground rules.
40. **S40 Red flag.** Controlling behaviour escalates to threats. Teaches: recognition and getting safe (see `10-red-lines-and-safety.md`).

## Writing standards for scenarios
- 120 to 180 words. Plain language, English with Urdu/Hindi terms where natural.
- Each has at least three options: the reactive response, the avoidant response, the skilled response.
- Show **both partners' inner view**. The same event is written twice.
- No villain. Even difficult relatives have understandable fears.
- Mark whether it is **universal** or **region-specific**.
