# 03. Journey Timeline

Seven phases. Each lists the goals, the conversations that must happen, and the traps.

## Phase 0: Rishta and decision (before the families commit)
**Goal:** make an informed choice, not only a socially approved one.
**Conversations:** career and money picture, religion in daily life, children and timing, where we live, working after marriage, previous relationships and obligations, health disclosures, family dependants.
**Traps:** deciding on family status and photos alone; "we'll sort it out after the wedding"; hiding a material fact.
**Output:** a **Deal-breakers and non-negotiables list** per person.

## Phase 1: Engagement period
**Goal:** the first real conversations between the two of them, with the families as a respectful frame.
**Conversations:** the expectations audit; the nikahnama conditions; mehr and what it means; division of wedding costs; dowry/gifts stance; honeymoon and first-months plan; first-year living arrangement.
**Traps:** long late-night phone calls that cover romance but no decisions; over-sharing pre-marital messages with relatives; each family pressing its own wishes.
**Output:** a signed-off **Pre-marriage agreements sheet** (private, not legal) and a **united-front script** for families.

## Phase 2: Wedding cycle (mehndi, nikah, barat, walima)
**Goal:** get through the events without damaging the relationship.
**Conversations:** who handles which relatives; budget stress; how to protect rest and a quiet moment; what the couple wants to say no to.
**Traps:** the couple is invisible in their own wedding; exhaustion and tension discharged at each other; public humiliation over dowry or gifts.
**Output:** a **wedding-week survival plan** and a protected first evening.

## Phase 3: Days 1 to 30 (the honeymoon is also an audit)
**Goal:** establish routines, learn each other, reduce shock.
**What happens:** new household, new rules, constant visitors, first visits to the bride's parents (the "pag phera"), first disagreements, intimacy adjustments, physical exhaustion.
**Do:** a quiet 30 minutes daily; ask "what's the hardest thing about today?"; agree on privacy (door, phone, room); introduce the weekly check-in; protect time with her parents.
**Traps:** the bride is expected to perform, the groom is expected to disappear into the family; the first big fight is in public.

## Phase 4: Months 2 to 4 (the reality phase)
**Goal:** convert initial politeness into honest, sustainable roles.
**What happens:** the guest rota ends and the household's real structure appears; money and chore conflicts; first major in-law friction; her return to work or study; loneliness for the bride, pressure on the groom.
**Do:** the first **money meeting**; clear the **roles** list; one proper conversation about the mother/mother-in-law dynamic with the couple's united front.
**Traps:** one partner wins every argument by pulling rank (money, age, family); venting to the wrong person.

## Phase 5: Months 5 to 8 (the pressure phase)
**Goal:** hold boundaries and keep intimacy alive.
**What happens:** "good news?" questions begin; sibling and relative politics; financial demands; career decisions; first relocation or separate-household discussion.
**Do:** the **five-year plan** review; an agreed answer on children timing; plan one weekend away; a **repair** conversation for the biggest unresolved hurt.
**Traps:** resentment stored up and unspoken; pregnancy pursued to satisfy others; career sacrificed silently.

## Phase 6: Months 9 to 12 (the maturity phase)
**Goal:** build a durable marriage structure.
**What happens:** the first anniversary; first-year review; decisions on housing, children, career; more mature family relationships.
**Do:** an **anniversary review** (what worked, what to change, what to stop); thank the people who helped; update agreements; schedule a counselling check-in even if things are fine.
**Traps:** assuming "we got through it, so we're fine"; ignoring a pattern that has become routine.

## Cross-phase: the support track
- Month 0, 1, 3, 6 and 12 **check-in forms** (self-assessment + couple reflection).
- A **"when to get help"** guide at each stage: counsellor, trusted elder, lawyer, medical professional, emergency services.
- A **"family reset"** conversation guide that can be used any time.
